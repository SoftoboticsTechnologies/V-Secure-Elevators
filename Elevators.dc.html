Safety enhancements for passengers

Elevator standards EN 81-20 and EN 81-50

UCM and overspeed

Unintended car movement with the doors open must be detected and stopped by an independent device. Overspeed protection acts in both directions of travel, upward as well as downward. Both systems are tested at commissioning and again at periodic inspection.

Door detection systems

Light curtains and edge detection cover the full height of the entrance rather than a single beam. The car doors reopen on obstruction without contact with the passenger. Detection is required to stay effective as the doors close at reduced speed.

Door locking mechanism

Landing doors stay locked until the car is within the unlocking zone at that floor. Interlocks are wired into the safety circuit so the lift cannot run with a door unlocked. Engagement of the locking element is specified to a minimum depth and verified on site.

Fire classification

Materials used in the car, doors and shaft are limited for flame spread, heat release and smoke production. Landing doors carry a fire rating suited to the building compartment they sit in. Firefighter and evacuation operation is set up where the building requires it.

Lighting

Car and landing lighting levels are specified so the controls, threshold and floor are clearly visible. Emergency lighting comes on automatically on supply failure and runs for a defined period. The machine room, pit and shaft carry permanent lighting for maintenance access.

Strength

Car walls, doors and roof are designed for defined static and impact loads, including pendulum tests. Guarding heights, refuge spaces in the pit and headroom protect technicians working in the shaft. Structural fixings and guide rails are calculated for the rated load and speed.
